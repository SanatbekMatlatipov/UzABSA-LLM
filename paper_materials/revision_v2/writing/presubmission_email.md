**Title:** UzABSA-LLM: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, Encoder Baselines, and Human-Validated
Multi-Domain Annotation for Uzbek Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis

**Abstract (~230 words):**
Aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) is well studied for high-resource languages but remains
largely unaddressed for Uzbek, a low-resource Turkic language with script duality and rich
agglutinative morphology. We present, to our knowledge, the first systematic study of
parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) of open-source large language models for Uzbek ABSA.
Three 7–8B models (Qwen 2.5, Llama 3.1, DeepSeek-R1-Distill) are fine-tuned under identical
configurations to jointly extract aspect terms and classify sentiment as structured JSON, and
are benchmarked against fine-tuned Uzbek BERT encoders. Beyond the model comparison, our central
contribution is a scalable, reproducible pipeline for low-resource ABSA data creation that
combines local LLM inference, LLM-as-Judge quality scoring, and quality-tiered assembly. We
apply it to 5,038 multi-domain Uzbek business reviews across 23 categories, producing a new
silver-standard resource of 9,884 aspect–sentiment pairs, and we validate a stratified sample
against native-speaker annotations — reporting inter-annotator agreement, calibration of the
LLM judge against human judgement, and a gold-verified subset. We further quantify how
extraction quality degrades with distance from the single training domain. The study offers
both a practical methodology for building NLP resources in low-resource settings and empirical
evidence on model and evaluation choices for structured-output tasks. All code, datasets, and
models are released publicly.

We believe this fits the Special Issue's themes of low-resource and multilingual NLP,
large language models, and sentiment analysis. Could you kindly advise whether it is suitable?
